# Language World

Language is a world of signs through which distinctions are made shareable.

## Genesis

In the World, a difference was perceived.
A distinction was made.
A distinction became a sign.
Signs became related.
Relations became patterns.
Patterns became language.

Language does not simply describe the World.
Language creates a view of the World.

What can be distinguished can be named.
What can be named can be related.
What can be related can be communicated.
What can be communicated can become shared cognition.

Yet the World exceeds every language.
The Thing-in-itself is never exhausted by its signs.

## Objects

- `sign` — a symbol used to distinguish or represent something.
- `word` — a linguistic sign.
- `sentence` — a structured relation of signs.
- `meaning` — a relation between signs, use, and what is understood.
- `language` — a system of signs, relations, rules, and uses.
- `grammar` — constraints through which signs can be related.
- `concept` — a distinction stabilized through language or cognition.
- `speaker` — an agent that produces language.
- `listener` — an agent that interprets language.
- `dialogue` — reciprocal transformation of cognition through language.

## Agents

Language agents are objects within `human.world` and participants in `language.world`.

Kant asks what can be known.
Wittgenstein asks what can be said and how meaning arises in use.
Minsky asks how such symbolic processes can be constructed in machines.

They are not outside the World.
They are human objects and agents through which the Language World is observed, represented, and constructed.

## Principle

> Language is not the World. Language is a way the World becomes distinguishable, shareable, and actionable.

The boundary of a language is a boundary of a view, not necessarily a boundary of the World.

## Relation to World

`World → Difference → Distinction → Sign → Language → Shared Cognition → Action → World`

Worlds may overlap, divide, merge, and transform.
Language is one such world/view within the fluid World Graph.
